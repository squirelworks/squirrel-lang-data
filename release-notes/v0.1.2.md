# squirrel-lang-data v0.1.2

The second update to the frozen data, made from the first round of testing
the apps. It corrects mistakes testers found, and it moves the last of the
SquirrelGo apps' own content here so every app reads from one place. Read
"Before you update" first: three Japanese cards lose their review history.

## Corrections

- **Japanese greetings placed at JLPT N5** (#36). こんにちは, こんばんは,
  さようなら, はじめまして, すみません, ごめんなさい, いただきます, ありがとう and
  おはよう were at N3 or N2, as in Waller's lists. They are now estimated at
  N5, which the JLPT describes as reading "typical expressions and sentences
  written in hiragana, katakana, and basic kanji". 美味しい was tagged N1 and
  is now N5, and 今日は / 今晩は read konnichiwa / konbanwa.
- **Kana example words fixed.** ぢしん and づくえ were misspellings of じしん and
  つくえ, ヂャズ should be ジャズ, たべ, らいとう, ぷる and ヤッツ are not words, and
  ぴアノ, ぺン and ぽスト mixed the two scripts. Hiragana examples now use words
  that are written in hiragana, and ヂ, ヅ and ヲ say they are rare in katakana.
- **Hangul ㄳ example fixed.** It was 값, whose final consonant is ㅄ; it is now 몫.
- **Chinese words no longer carry guessed HSK levels** (#41). The levels
  were guessed from how often a word appears in mostly news text, which put
  政府 (government) at HSK 1 and 喝 (drink) at HSK 4. Words keep their
  frequency tags. HSK levels for the starter words will be placed by hand
  against the official level descriptions in a later release.

## New

- **Stroke order and script courses moved here.** The stroke files for 35
  scripts, and the script courses from SquirrelGo Alphabets, Kana, Bopomofo
  and Klingon, now live in `strokes/` and `alphabets/`, with their schemas,
  checks and generators. The apps will read them from here instead of keeping
  their own copies.
- **Frequency tags for 11 more languages:** Arabic, Bengali, Russian, French,
  German, Greek, Hebrew, Hindi, Italian, Taiwanese and Tamil vocabulary are
  now tagged by how common each word is, as Japanese, Korean and Chinese
  already were.
- **Inflection tables** (`inflections@1`). A language can list each word's
  forms, such as polite or past, so a translator can go from a form back to
  the dictionary word and the other way. It starts with nine Japanese
  verbs.
- **Two more language pack formats.** Every language can now also be
  exported as an Anki deck package, and as a Yomitan dictionary for
  looking up words while reading in a browser.

## Before you update

- **Three Japanese cards lose their review history in the Vocabulary
  Learner.** The Desk stores progress per deck and card text, and three
  cards changed deck: ありがとう (JLPT N3 → N5), おはよう (JLPT N2 → N5) and
  おいしい (JLPT N5, now the Food card 美味しい). They come back as new
  cards. Nothing else is lost.

## Not in this release

The HSK level method for Chinese is still being decided and will come in a
later release.
