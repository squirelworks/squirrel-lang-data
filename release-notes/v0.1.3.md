# squirrel-lang-data v0.1.3

The third update to the frozen data, from the second round of testing. The
Chinese, Japanese and Korean columns of the meaning matrix now cover every
sentence, so the translators have far more to work with. Chinese words drop
the HSK levels that were guessed from frequency, and Korean grammar's
placeholder lessons are written properly.

## Meaning matrix

- **The Chinese column renders every sentence.** It had 20 of the 89
  sentences; the other 69 are added, written for this project, so words
  such as 喜欢 (like), 爱 (love), 知道 (know) and 看见 (see) are now in the
  matrix and translators can use them.
- **The Japanese column renders every sentence.** It had 21 of the 89
  sentences; the other 68 are added, written for this project, in the
  same polite style as the existing rows, with "I" and "you" left implied
  where Japanese usually drops them.
- **The Korean column renders every sentence.** It had 20 of the 89
  sentences; the other 69 are added, written for this project, in the
  polite 해요 style of the existing rows, with each particle's form
  matching the word before it (책을, 차를).

## Corrections

- **Chinese words no longer carry guessed HSK levels** (#41). The levels
  were guessed from how often a word appears in mostly news text, which put
  政府 (government) at HSK 1 and 喝 (drink) at HSK 4. Words keep their
  frequency tags.
- **The 250 Chinese starter words carry estimated HSK levels again**, this
  time placed by hand (#41). Each word gets the lowest HSK 3.0 level whose
  official description and topic list would need it: 195 at level 1, 49 at
  level 2 and 6 at level 3. These are estimates of the level a word probably
  falls under, not a copy of the official word list, which carries no reuse
  license. The Chinese reference card on HSK levels quotes the official
  descriptions. The large frequency decks still carry no HSK level.
- **Korean grammar: 16 stub lessons rewritten** (#49). Sixteen of the 72
  Korean lessons were placeholders, with a single unromanized example and
  filler drills ("Pattern level", "Korean puts… verb last"). Five of their
  fill-in drills doubled the ending, so the "right" answer read 온다고다고.
  Each now has a real explanation, romanized examples and drills that test
  the pattern. Two were duplicates of existing lessons and three overlapped
  others, so those five now teach the next pattern in their slot: -(으)면서,
  -(으)면 되다, polite N이/가 어떻게 되세요?, notice language, and reported
  questions, orders and suggestions. Three slips elsewhere are fixed too:
  combined vowels wrap under and to the right of the consonant (not only to
  its right), 예 is no longer marked a wrong "yes", and 커피이다 is no longer
  called wrong in the plain style.
- **Hangul stroke order fixed for five letters.** ㅓ and ㅕ drew the long
  vertical first; the short strokes come first. ㅎ drew the bar before the
  top tick. ㅅ had a stray third stroke (it has two), and ㅆ's first stroke
  ran from one ㅅ into the other (it has four). The shapes are unchanged;
  only the order and the stroke breaks moved.
