# squirrel-lang-data v0.1.5

Draft. Everything merged since v0.1.4, collected as it lands.

The meaning matrix gains three new languages and fills three more, so
squirrelator's Pipeline and the Translation Learner can work end to end in
French, German, Italian, Latin, Greek and Russian. English sources with
contractions now match their rows, and Aramaic gets the Syriac alphabet.

## Meaning matrix

- **French, German and Italian columns** (#105). All 103 rows each (the 89
  sentences and the 14 code-pack instructions), written for this project.
  French and Italian elision glues to the next word (j', l', dell'),
  hyphenated inversions such as est-il are one token, and contractions
  carry both meanings (au, sul). Code rows use the infinitive, the usual
  form for software instructions in all three (Afficher le mot., Das Wort
  ausgeben., Stampare la parola.).
- **Latin, Greek and Russian filled to every row** (#105). Latin from 52
  to 103 rows, with Neo-Latin words where Classical Latin has none
  (cafēum, autocinētum, laophorum, trāmen); Greek from 56 to 103; Russian
  from 34 to 103, dropping the present-tense copula as Russian does.
- **A grammar meaning for the partitive** (#105). `@partitive` ("some")
  for French du and de l' and Italian dell'.
- **English contractions** (#105). don't, doesn't, didn't, isn't, aren't,
  wasn't, can't and won't carry not; let's, I'm, we're and you're carry
  let-us, i, we and you. "I don't understand." and "Let's go." used to
  come back with the contraction in brackets in every language.
- **More English forms** (#105). rain as a verb (raining) and Klingon as a
  noun (Klingons).

With squirrelator main (#48), every full column now comes back as its
English, and every English row goes into the column, for at least 99 of
103 rows (squirelworks/squirrelator#26).

## Characters

- **The Syriac alphabet** (#106). A 22-letter course in the Estrangela
  style for SquirrelGo Alphabets, with names, academic romanization,
  Classical Syriac IPA, an example noun per letter, the letters that never
  join forward (which turns on the letter forms table), and articles on
  the script, joining and its three styles. Its strokes are a machine
  draft from Noto Sans Syriac that passes the stroke check; they still
  want a review against a writing primer. This is the last open item of
  LanguageStudentDesk#201.

## Not in this release

- Columns for the languages that still have none, and the rest of the rows
  for Arabic, Hindi, Hebrew, Thai, Georgian, Armenian, Bengali, Tamil and
  Klingon.
- More grammar for Aramaic, Cherokee, Inuktitut, Manipuri and Tamazight,
  which needs published sources.
